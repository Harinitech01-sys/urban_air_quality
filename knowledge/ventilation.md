# Knowledge Base: Winter Smog Inversion & Ventilation Timing

## 1. Ground-Level Thermal Inversion Dynamics
- Under normal weather conditions, atmospheric temperature decreases with altitude, allowing warm surface air to rise and carry pollutants away.
- In winter, ground radiation cooling during clear calm nights cools the near-surface air faster than the air aloft.
- This creates an **inversion cap**: cold, dense air remains trapped under a warm layer, restricting the planetary boundary layer height to 150 - 300 meters.
- Surface emissions (vehicle exhaust, brick kilns, biomass burning) accumulate rapidly, leading to severe smog spikes between 6:00 AM – 9:30 AM and 7:30 PM – 11:00 PM.

## 2. When to Ventilate vs. When to Seal
- **STRICT SEAL WINDOWS**:
  - Early Morning (5:00 AM – 10:00 AM): Severe inversion and traffic rush hour.
  - Night (7:00 PM – 6:00 AM): Surface cooling traps evening emissions.
- **OPTIMAL VENTILATION WINDOW**:
  - Midday (12:00 PM – 3:30 PM): Solar heating warms the ground, breaking the nocturnal inversion and expanding the boundary layer height up to 800 - 1,200 meters.
  - Wind speed typically peaks in the afternoon, providing maximum atmospheric dilution.
  - If indoor CO2 levels rise above 1,000 ppm, execute brief 10 to 15-minute cross-ventilation during this midday window only.
