# Knowledge Base: Indoor Air Quality & Air Purifiers

## 1. Purifier Sizing & CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)
- CADR (measured in CFM or $m^3/h$) represents the volume of clean air delivered per unit time.
- Standard AHAM / ASHRAE Rule of Thumb:
  - $\text{Required CADR (CFM)} \ge \text{Room Area (sq ft)} \times 2/3$.
  - $\text{Required CADR } (m^3/h) \ge \text{Room Volume } (m^3) \times 4.0\ \text{ACH}$.
- Target Air Changes per Hour (ACH):
  - Normal conditions: 3.0 to 4.0 ACH.
  - Severe winter smog (AQI > 300) or vulnerable individuals (asthma/COPD): **4.0 to 6.0 ACH**.

## 2. DIY Corsi-Rosenthal Box Fan Filter
- Developed by Dr. Richard Corsi and Jim Rosenthal; verified by UC Davis and EPA test chambers.
- Materials:
  - 1x 20-inch standard box fan (~$20 - $25)
  - 4x MERV-13 pleated HVAC furnace filters (20" x 20" x 2") (~$40)
  - 1x Cardboard bottom piece and duct tape
  - 1x Cardboard shroud on fan face (reduces backflow and boosts CFM by 15%)
- Performance:
  - Delivers CADR of 600 - 850 $m^3/h$ (comparable to $500 commercial HEPA units) for approximately $60 total cost.
  - Quiet operation on speed 1 or 2; lasts 6 months during winter smog.

## 3. Technologies to Avoid
- **Ozone Generators**: Marketed as "air fresheners" or "purifiers", ozone is a potent lung toxin that damages pulmonary epithelium and exacerbates asthma.
- **Ionizers**: Emit small amounts of ozone and charge particles so they stick to walls, without effectively capturing them.
- **Houseplants Myth**: NASA clean air studies were conducted in airtight sealed chambers under high intensity lighting; in real homes with 0.5-1.0 ACH air leakage, thousands of plants would be required to equal a single small HEPA filter.
